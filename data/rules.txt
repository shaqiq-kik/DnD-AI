The core mechanics of Dungeons and Dragons fifth edition are built entirely upon the d20 system. Whenever a character attempts an action where the outcome is uncertain, the player rolls a twenty-sided die, adds the relevant ability modifier and proficiency bonus, and compares the total against a target number. For ability checks and saving throws, this target number is called a Difficulty Class, or DC. A standard scale is used by Dungeon Masters to set these DCs: an easy task has a DC of 10, a medium difficulty task has a DC of 15, and a hard task has a DC of 20.

A central mechanic influencing these rolls is advantage and disadvantage. If a character has a significant situational upper hand, they roll with advantage, meaning they roll two d20s and keep the higher result. Conversely, if circumstances are working against them, they roll with disadvantage, rolling two d20s and keeping the lower result.

Combat begins with an initiative roll to determine the turn order. Everyone involved rolls a Dexterity check, and the combatants act in descending order from the highest result to the lowest. When a character attacks, they make an attack roll by rolling a d20 and adding their attack bonus. This total is compared to the target's Armor Class, or AC. If the total equals or exceeds the AC, the attack hits, and the attacker rolls for damage.

Saving throws represent a character's attempt to resist negative effects, such as dodging a trap or fighting off a poison. The DM calls for a specific ability saving throw, and the player rolls a d20, adding their saving throw modifier, hoping to meet or beat the spell or trap's specific DC.

Outside of combat, skill checks dictate the success of exploration and social interaction. Stealth checks are rolled using Dexterity when a character attempts to move silently and remain hidden from enemies. Persuasion checks use Charisma to influence others through diplomacy, negotiation, or charm. Perception checks are based on Wisdom and determine what a character notices in their environment, such as hidden doors or ambushing foes. Athletics checks utilize Strength to handle physical challenges like climbing sheer cliffs, swimming against strong currents, or grappling opponents. These core mechanics weave together to adjudicate every heroic action and perilous danger the characters face.
